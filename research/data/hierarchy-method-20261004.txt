This is a reasonable, tightly scoped experiment for testing whether explicitly separating CENTER from partisan text improves the baseline’s weak CENTER recall (4/26, about 15%).

- **Leakage control:** Fit both models within each frozen fold. Reuse that fold’s existing training-only TF-IDF transformation, including IDF weights—not just its vocabulary. Train the LEFT/RIGHT model only on partisan training rows.
- **Balanced weighting:** Apply balancing separately in each binary task. This makes the CENTER detector more sensitive to the minority class, but may increase false CENTER predictions. Its predicted probabilities should not be assumed calibrated.
- **Gate behavior:** The fixed 0.5 gate is a clear, reproducible decision rule. CENTER-gate errors are irreversible: partisan rows sent to CENTER never reach the LEFT/RIGHT decision, and missed CENTER rows must become LEFT or RIGHT.
- **Joint scores:** The proposed scores are nonnegative and sum to one. That establishes coherence, not calibration. Clearly distinguish hard-gate predictions from joint-score argmax predictions; report the requested metrics using the hard gate.
- **Comparison:** Use pooled OOF predictions on exactly the same 115 rows and folds as the frozen baseline. Report accuracy, macro-F1, each class’s recall, and coverage (expected 115/115). Include the confusion matrix and CENTER precision to show whether recall gains come from overpredicting CENTER.

Interpret cautiously: each additional correctly classified CENTER row changes its recall by about 3.85 percentage points. With only 26 CENTER examples and five event-string folds, gains may depend heavily on particular events; event-string separation also need not eliminate related-event or source overlap. Because the baseline weakness motivated this experiment, these OOF results are development evidence, not independent confirmation. Any improvement supports this combined hierarchy-and-weighting intervention; it does not isolate which component helped.
