Labeling rules

Classify the text's political framing, not the publisher or your agreement with it. Read the whole text. Separate quotations from authorial endorsement.

  • LEFT / RIGHT: sustained authorial framing supports corresponding U.S. ideological positions. Mentions and quotations alone are insufficient.
  • CENTER: enough relevant context exists and the framing is substantively nonaligned, balanced or centrist. It does not mean factually correct.
  • NONPOLITICAL: no substantive political content. Direction words or everyday uses of “liberal” and “conservative” are not political evidence.
  • UNCERTAIN: context is insufficient, positions are irreconcilably mixed, attribution is unclear, or the U.S. ideology scheme does not apply.

Record relevance separately. Political procedural reporting can be CENTER; an isolated fragment may be UNCERTAIN. Consult RUBRIC.md for the complete instructions. All 100 items are pilot development material, not a final test.
